# Executive Summary for Government Stakeholders — European Banking Churn Analytics

## Scope and snapshot

This descriptive analysis covers **10,000 customer records** from the supplied 2025 snapshot. It measures observed churn patterns and balance exposure; the data does not establish causal drivers or customer revenue.

## Portfolio KPIs

- Customers: 10,000
- Churned: 2,037 (20.4%)
- Retained: 7,963
- Average account balance: 76,485.89
- Churned-customer balance exposure: 185,588,094.63 (24.3% of observed portfolio balance)
- High-balance proxy churn rate: 23.0%
- High-balance proxy share of churners: 18.0%

## Observed patterns

- Highest country churn rate: **Germany**, 32.4% across 2,509 customers; churn contribution is 40.0%.
- Highest age-segment churn rate: **46–60**, 51.1% across 1,647 customers.
- Highest tenure-group churn rate: **New**, 21.2% across 2,496 customers.
- The high-balance proxy includes 1,596 customers; 367 churned, with 23.0% churn and 58,714,368.09 of balance exposure held by those churners.

## Retention priorities for evaluation

### Germany has an elevated observed churn rate.

**Evidence:** 814 of 2,509 customers churned (32.4%), versus 20.4% overall.

**Suggested next step:** Review this segment's service, product, and engagement experience before designing a targeted retention test.

### Customers aged 46–60 have an elevated observed churn rate.

**Evidence:** 842 of 1,647 customers churned (51.1%), versus 20.4% overall.

**Suggested next step:** Review this segment's service, product, and engagement experience before designing a targeted retention test.

### Customers with 3 product(s) have an elevated observed churn rate.

**Evidence:** 220 of 266 customers churned (82.7%), versus 20.4% overall.

**Suggested next step:** Review this segment's service, product, and engagement experience before designing a targeted retention test.

### Customers in Germany aged 46–60 have an elevated observed churn rate.

**Evidence:** 338 of 502 customers churned (67.3%), versus 20.4% overall.

**Suggested next step:** Review the customer journey for this combination and evaluate a targeted retention test before scaling any intervention.

### Inactive members have a higher observed churn rate than active members.

**Evidence:** Inactive: 26.9% (n=4,849); active: 14.3% (n=5,151).

**Suggested next step:** Test proactive engagement outreach and measure subsequent retention before scaling it.

## Interpretation limits

- This is a single-period customer snapshot with no transaction, service-contact, product-profitability, or historical event data.
- Churn rates describe observed association. They should not be interpreted as causal effects or validated future churn probabilities.
- Account balances are shown as balance exposure, not revenue, profit, or recoverable value.
- The high-value cohort is a transparent high-balance proxy; it is not a complete customer lifetime-value measure.
